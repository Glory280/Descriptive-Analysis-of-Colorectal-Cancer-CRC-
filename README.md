# 📊 Descriptive Analysis of Colorectal Cancer (CRC)

### A Public Health Perspective

This repository contains a descriptive epidemiological analysis of colorectal cancer patient demographics and tumor characteristics. The project focuses on summarizing patterns in age, sex, ethnicity, and tumor location using **Power BI**, with a strong public health lens.

---

## 🧠 Project Overview

Colorectal cancer (CRC) remains a major global public health challenge. Understanding *who* is affected and *how* the disease presents is a crucial first step for surveillance, screening strategies, and health equity planning.

This project analyzes a dataset of **225 colorectal cancer patients** using descriptive statistics and visualizations. The goal is insight, not causation.

---

## 🎯 Objectives

* Describe the **age distribution** of CRC patients
* Examine **sex distribution** within the dataset
* Assess **ethnic representation** among patients
* Identify the most common **primary tumor locations**
* Explore the presence of **early-onset colorectal cancer** cases


---

## 📊 Data Source & Methods

### Dataset

* **Sample size:** 225 CRC patients
* **Variables analyzed:**

  * Age at diagnosis
  * Sex
  * Ethnicity
  * Primary tumor location

### Analytical Approach

* Tool used: **Power BI**
* Methods: Descriptive statistics only
* Visualizations:

  * Bar charts
  * Donut charts
  * Histograms

All results are presented as **counts, percentages, and distributions**.

---

## 🔍 Key Findings

### Age Distribution

* Majority of cases occurred in patients **≥50 years**
* **12 patients** were diagnosed before age 40

**Takeaway:** CRC is mainly an older-adult disease, but early-onset cases exist and matter.

---

### Sex Distribution

* **Males** represented a higher proportion of CRC cases than females

This aligns with established global epidemiological trends.

---

### Ethnicity Distribution

* Dataset is predominantly composed of **White patients**
* Other ethnic groups are underrepresented

**Public health note:** Limited diversity may affect generalizability.

---

### Tumor Location

* **Colon tumors** were more common than rectal tumors

This suggests a higher burden of colon cancer within the studied population.

---

## 🧩 Discussion

The findings reflect known CRC patterns: higher prevalence among older adults and males, with colon cancer being more common than rectal cancer. While early-onset cases are relatively rare, their presence supports growing global concern around CRC in younger populations.

However, the dataset’s lack of ethnic diversity highlights an important limitation when interpreting results through a health equity lens.

---

## 🌍 Public Health Implications

* Reinforces the importance of **CRC screening from age 50+**
* Supports increasing awareness of **early-onset CRC**
* Highlights the need for **representative datasets** in public health research

Descriptive analyses like this are foundational for surveillance and policy planning.

---

## ⚠️ Limitations

* Descriptive analysis only (no causal inference)
* Moderate sample size
* Limited ethnic diversity
* No analysis of outcomes, risk factors, or survival

---

## ✅ Conclusion

This project provides a clear, data-driven snapshot of colorectal cancer demographics and tumor location from a public health perspective. The results mirror established epidemiological trends and create a solid base for future analytical or inferential studies in CRC research.
